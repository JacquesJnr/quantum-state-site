# Compare Against the Problem

Quantum State is measured against anyone solving the same problems, sovereign AI, connected governance data, trustworthy AI output, not only against board software.

Board software vendors show what directors already buy: cited prep questions, pack summaries, human review. Those features are already taken. The harder comparison sits in other markets: sovereign AI vendors running models on-premises or air-gapped at scale, and knowledge-graph platforms that connect an organization's data and reason over it for regulated buyers. Their successes show what already works and what it costs to run; their failures, output given more authority than its evidence earned, show where the risk sits.

This rules out declaring success by matching a board-software feature list. Citations, human review and an "AI-native" label don't set anything apart on their own if the category leader already ships them. It also rules out ignoring a failure just because it happened outside board governance: a citation scandal or an unprofitable enterprise AI program teaches the same lesson wherever it happens.

The question this principle keeps asking is not "does this beat Diligent," but "does this hold up against whoever has already tried to solve sovereign AI, connected data, or trustworthy output, anywhere."
